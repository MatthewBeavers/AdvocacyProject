Hi my name is Matthew Beavers, and this is my advocacy project to enhance the Seattle University dining experience. For students that are in university for a significant portion of the year, the dining experience feels lacking. Not only that, but college students often face food insecurity, and according to a scholarly article published in the International Journal of Health, Wellness & Society states that “about 17 percent of the student population identified themselves to be food insecure.” (Jesch, Elizabeth, et al. 2021) That is many students, and if you were to apply this number to Seattle University’s undergraduate population, that would be approximately 860 students. Which is quite a large number of the student population. In fact, it actually turns out that 40% of Seattle University Students face or have faced food insecurity on campus, including myself. So, I believe Seattle University should enhance/create more programs to help students with food insecurity, and to make the dining experience better.

Resources

Seattle University thankfully already offers some resources for those who face challenges with food. But I feel that the resources should be advertised more. We currently have the food pantry, where students can get free groceries, but the process requires you to fill out an application, which some can feel embarrassing. There is also the case that some students may only need temporary help, and filling out a full application can feel arduous. There is the food recovery program, which makes ready to eat meals available, but those are only available at certain locations like the military/veteran link, or the commuter link. I think this program could be enhanced to provide greater access to ready-to-eat meals for the student population. One way to do this is to have the same program run out of The Cave at Campion Hall. This would allow students facing food insecurity to go to a place they are comfortable with, to get a ready-to-eat meal when they need it. Resources

about page

My name is Matthew Beavers, and I am a sophomore at Seattle University. I was inspired to create this project because I have Celiac disease and am also allergic to cow milk. This project was designed for Seattle University students. I was also inspired to create this project because of the book Revolutions a Very Short Introduction by Goldstone. I read this book for my UCOR 3400 class, and while reading it I noticed that there were two elements of revolution from the book that could be applied to this project. The first is fiscal strains, given that we are college students and during a time when the economy due to the current United States administration is suffering. (Goldstone, 2023) The second is “increasingly widespread popular anger at injustice.” (Goldstone, 2023) Many feel like they are being scammed with the dining experience at Seattle University, and the more time I spend at this university, the more I notice how upset people are with dining. So I have taken it upon myself to try to be an advocate for Seattle University students, and while I cannot speak for everyone, I will do my best to keep everyone’s best interests at heart.

Moving Forward

So, how do we address change? There are a couple of different options that Seattle University could take. One study from a California school found that students express “a desire for practical financial and food literacy “life skills’ training” (Watson, et al. 2016) Given the Jesuit nature of our university, with the value of cura personalis, Seattle University could offer a training on food literacy during orientation. This could include budgeting, where to find food resources on campus like the ready-to-eat meals, and perhaps an expediated application for the food pantry at the training as well. Like I say in the resources section, there could also be free ready-to-eat meals at The Cave. Some other alternative solutions could be decreasing the overall cost of the food and increasing hours. I know that would be very difficult, given the current state of the economy and prices of food, but given the high tuiton costs for the school, many students including myself feel like we are being taken advantaged of due to the required on campus housing and meal plan for freshmen and sophomores. Even more so when the precieved value for cost of the food is low. As for increasing hours, most students spend their breaks on campus, so dining should be prepared for that, and not close all but two locations that run for limited hours on the breaks. For people who have jobs, these inconsistent hours make it near impossible to eat on campus during breaks, and often have to go else where and spend extra money on food when they shouldn’t have to. There is the counter point that students should be able to prepare meals for themselves, many students have difficulty accessing a kitchen, and may not have the proper supplies, and when they go to borrow supplies they are typically unavailable or extremely limited. One of the students I interviewed said that when they borrowed kitchen supplies, they recieved a singular saucepan and nothing else, which is hardly adequate to properly cook with.
