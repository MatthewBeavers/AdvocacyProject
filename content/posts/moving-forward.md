So, how do we address change? There are a couple of different options that Seattle University could take. One study from a California school found that students express “a desire for practical financial and food literacy “life skills' training” (Watson, et al. 2016) Given the Jesuit nature of our university, with the value of cura personalis, Seattle University could offer a training on food literacy during orientation. This could include budgeting, where to find food resources on campus like the ready-to-eat meals, and perhaps an expediated application for the food pantry at the training as well. Like I say in the resources section, there could also be free ready-to-eat meals at The Cave.
Some other alternative solutions could be decreasing the overall cost of the food and increasing hours. I know that would be very difficult, given the current state of the economy and prices of food, but given the high tuiton costs for the school, many students including myself feel like we are being taken advantaged of due to the required on campus housing and meal plan for freshmen and sophomores. Even more so when the precieved value for cost of the food is low. As for increasing hours, most students spend their breaks on campus, so dining should be prepared for that, and not close all but two locations that run for limited hours on the breaks. For people who have jobs, these inconsistent hours make it near impossible to eat on campus during breaks, and often have to go else where and spend extra money on food when they shouldn't have to.
There is the counter point that students should be able to prepare meals for themselves, many students have difficulty accessing a kitchen, and may not have the proper supplies, and when they go to borrow supplies they are typically unavailable or extremely limited. One of the students I interviewed said that when they borrowed kitchen supplies, they recieved a singular saucepan and nothing else, which is hardly adequate to properly cook with.
